How it works

Ask a question, get the newspapers' own words

The Time Capsule is a lightweight retrieval system (the "R" in RAG) over OCR text from historical newspapers. Ask a question and it finds the passages that answer it, quotes the most relevant sentence from each, orders them in time, and links every quote back to the scanned page, down to the printed column.

It is built for research: it never writes text the newspapers did not print, it shows how clean the OCR is, it tolerates OCR misspellings, and it spreads results across newspapers so one large daily cannot drown out the rest. Its India desk brings in the Indian press and the Gazette of India from this project's own archive. .

Search tips

  • Use period vocabulary: "wireless", "the grippe", "aeroplane".
  • Names and places work best: "Carpathia", "Johnstown".
  • Narrow by date to follow a story day by day.
  • The India desk is searchable in English and Hindi (the Gazette is bilingual).
  • Likely OCR misreadings of your words are matched automatically.

Newspaper pages and OCR courtesy of the Library of Congress, Chronicling America (a partnership with the National Endowment for the Humanities) and the Internet Archive. The historical newspapers were all published before 1931. The India desk adds headlines and links from Indian news outlets (2020) and an issue of the Gazette of India (2000) from this project's own archive.
